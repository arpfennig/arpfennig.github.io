I am a population geneticist and computational biologist. I develop population genetic theory and computational methods for structural variation and pangenomes, and use them to study how archaic introgression and admixture shaped human genomes.

### Current work

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher in the [Akey Lab](https://akeylab.princeton.edu/) at Princeton University. My postdoctoral work focuses on making the full spectrum of human genetic variation, especially structural variation, accessible to population and statistical genetics. I developed LongHap, a long-read phasing method that improves haplotype reconstruction of INDELs and structural variants and integrates the methylation signal already present in PacBio and Oxford Nanopore data. I am now extending these ideas to phased de novo genome assembly, developing population-genetic models tailored to the mutational processes of structural variants, and building methods that perform population-genetic analyses directly on pangenome graphs. 

In parallel, I develop spatially explicit models and simulation-based, machine-learning methods, including graph neural networks applied to ancestral recombination graphs, to infer population history across space and time.

### Background

I received my Ph.D. in Quantitative Biosciences from the Georgia Institute of Technology with [Joseph Lachance](https://popgen.gatech.edu/). My dissertation, *Theoretical and Empirical Population Genetics of Admixture and Introgression*, combined theory and large-scale data analysis: I developed a model showing how a heterogeneous hybrid genetic background changes the fate of introgressed alleles, tested its predictions in 30,780 recently admixed genomes, and showed how uncertainty affects estimates of sex-biased admixture from X-chromosomal and autosomal ancestry. I also contributed to studies of the genetic architecture of prostate cancer and androgenetic alopecia in men of African ancestry.

Before that, I earned an M.S. at Georgia Tech in the lab of Mark Borodovsky, where I developed MgCod for gene prediction in phage genomes that use multiple genetic codes, and a B.S. in Biomedical Sciences at Reutlingen University.
